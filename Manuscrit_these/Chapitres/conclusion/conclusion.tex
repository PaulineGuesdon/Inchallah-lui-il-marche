\paragraph{Chapter 1}
In this thesis I have presented in the first chapter all the physical concepts concerning the cooling scheme of our experiment
giving the sufficient elements for a new PhD -and similar cold atoms experiment- to lead the experiment and understand the 
physics behind it. 
Additionnally to the previous thesis on our team a new optical setup is presented in mine for the filtering of the ECDL MOGlabs
laser that is used for the PA process. The aim of the filtering cavity of this setup is to avoid spontaneous emission on detuned 
states because of the broadening of the laser spectrum that comes with amplified spontaneous emission.

\paragraph{Chapter 2}
The second chapter the results of our team article published in PRX QUANTUM during my PhD.
These research show the quantum plateform we engineered to realize measurement of physical and quantum devices of our Fermi gas.
This article starts with the characterisation of the pseudos-spin qubits we managed to inhance: their coherence time
reaches more than three seconds thanks to the low sensitivity of the $^{87}Sr$ in the ground state. 

Then the robustess of the pseudo-spin qubits enabled us to extend to a multi-state interferometer --thanks to the large Hilbert space
accessible from the nuclear spin I = 9/2 of the $^{87}Sr$-- that gives access to physical
quantities such as the linear and quadratic part of the lightshift induced for the two-photon Raman process of spin manipulation.

Finally one demonstrate the measurement scheme of non-commuting observables $\hat{s}^x, \hat{s}^y, \hat{s}^z$ that also translates as 
the production of Positive-Operator-Valued-Measures (POVMs) that I demonstrate the equivalence after the included article.

\paragraph{Chapter 3}
In the last chapter, I present the results of photoassociation $^{87}Sr$ unexplored yet.
The chapter opens with detailed material on molecular physics necessary to understand the measurements on the spectroscopic lines completed
with the analysis of the temperature and the decay of the atom number with the illumination time.
We went through numerous technical problems that are described in this chapter to which the reader can refer to and mostly should be aware
for spectroscopy measurements more generally.

These lines show a surprising shape on their strength and linewidth. The most plausible interpretation of these numbers is the predissociation
phenomenon of free-atoms excited to a vibrational state close in energy with an atomic potential to which it is coupled: then the atoms are not
bound through photoassociation but decay to an atomic kinetic continuum.
